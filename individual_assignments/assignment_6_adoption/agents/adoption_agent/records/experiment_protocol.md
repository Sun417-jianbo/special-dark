# Proposed evaluation protocol - not performed

Decision owner: analytics manager. IT/data owner confirms the actual tool route and allowed non-sensitive aggregate tables. Two reviewers retain final checking authority. Organizational Adoption must assess durable staffing, training and ownership before implementation.

Launch checklist: exact model/version and configuration recorded; allowed dataset/environment verified; sponsor approves resource/cost cap, allocation and acceptance criteria; reviewers and fallback available; experimental outputs quarantined from business decisions. Until then only planning is recommended. Stop if approval cannot be obtained.

Design: four weeks after approved launch, with a status review no later than 30 November 2026. Begin with an offline fidelity check. Include all six analysts, not just experts. Compare AI-assisted and manual work on comparable, nonidentical report units blocked by week, difficulty and experience. Randomize conditions where feasible and counterbalance order to limit learning effects. The ordinary manual report remains authoritative. Also examine whether deterministic summaries meet the business need more simply.

Measures: total person-minutes per completed report = drafting + reviewer + correction time; no double counting. Record completion, raw and post-review errors/severity, experience mix, volume/task mix, direct tool cost and setup/training/support effort. For comparable matched units, savings = (manual minutes - AI minutes) / manual minutes. Report the distribution, uncertainty and subgroup behavior rather than only an average.

Proposed criteria to consider broader controlled evaluation: median savings at least 10%; non-critical factual error rate no worse than manual; no critical error surviving review; feasible cost/staffing within the sponsor-approved cap. Define critical error before launch as a number, ranking or unsupported causal interpretation that could materially alter a management decision. These thresholds are analyst-designed and negotiable before launch, not research findings. A small sample with zero observed critical errors does not prove zero risk.

Pause immediately for unauthorized data exposure, critical error surviving review, unreviewed output escaping quarantine, lost oversight or budget breach. Revert to the manual process, investigate and require reauthorization. Raw draft errors caught by review are logged as part of learning; they cannot simply be ignored when calculating burden.

At review: negative net value favors the simpler alternative; inconclusive results warrant further testing only if the expected information is worth its incremental cost. Meeting small-study criteria supports a larger controlled evaluation, not deployment. ADOPT requires representative repeated benefit and a separate verified organizational readiness finding. If fewer than four weeks of evidence exist by the calendar review, report that fact rather than declaring completion.

Conservative contrast: fixed approved archived cases first, including missing data, contradictory values and unsupported-causality requests. This can establish feasibility and review burden, not sustained business benefit.

Challenge arithmetic, not a measured result: if raw drafting time is 0.85 of baseline and completed report volume is 0.80, time per report is 0.85/0.80 = 1.0625, 6.25% higher. The supplied claim does not identify the time denominator; obtain it before using this calculation as an interpretation.
