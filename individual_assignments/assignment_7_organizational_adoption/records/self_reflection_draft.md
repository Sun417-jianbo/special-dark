The most useful distinction for me is that a technology can work while the organization is still not ready to use it reliably. In my earlier readiness answers, I focused on missing training, conflicting knowledge-base content and dependence on one employee. This assignment applies that thinking to a more specific decision: who will review the AI findings, who will maintain the process and what happens when the main person is unavailable?

I would not treat a good result from two experienced employees as enough for a wider rollout. I would involve people with different experience levels and look at the time needed for checking and correcting the AI's work. A named owner also needs time, authority and a backup; otherwise the responsibility exists only on paper.

My main professional takeaway is to ask for the evidence that could change the decision. If review capacity or ongoing funding is unknown, I should ask for those details instead of assuming they exist. AI helped organize the framework and test cases, but I still need to decide whether its recommendations address the actual gaps.
