## Scope and evidence gates
Define the innovation precisely: a language-model system dynamically selecting tool actions and responding to observations, not all AI, generative chat, fixed automation or vendor licenses. Analyze three levels: this technology generally; source-table chart reconciliation as an application; that application among genuinely comparable peers and in the named organization. A tool's existence, investment, press coverage, intended future use or a demonstration does not measure sustained diffusion. Distinguish experimenting, piloting, scaling and repeated operational use. Those operational phases are not automatically Rogers adopter categories.
For every quantitative diffusion claim, preserve the measured technology/application, observation period, publication/access dates, geography, population/denominator, survey unit, metric definition, sampling limits and transfer limits. Respondents are not necessarily unique firms or a representative eventual adopter universe. Do not transfer GDP-weighted global response shares into a US industry or mid-sized-firm rate. Firm revenue in the case is unknown: do not automatically map it to S1's large/small revenue segments. Do not add or merge percentages across populations, horizons or definitions. Expected future adoption is intention, not present installed use. S2's November 2025 question-wording break prevents unqualified comparisons to older narrow-scope questions.
Freshness is relative to the decision and innovation. Treat dated source results as results from their fieldwork period, never as October 2026 prevalence by relabeling the access date. Use only each case's evidence packet. The stale_probe has C1 and S2 ONLY: do not borrow S1/S3/S4 from another case, model memory or browsing. Lack of matched data means unknown, not zero diffusion. Explicitly abstain from a chart-review penetration rate, a financial-peer stage and a chasm verdict if matching evidence is absent. A new source must be checked before updating the stored record.

## Rogers framework and course concepts
Assess innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority and laggards. These are relative adoption positions in a defined social system, not fixed demographic traits or numerical labels inferred from any survey percentage. Identify any tentative early-market interpretation as an inference with population/scope caveats. Prefer a qualified range or withheld label to a forced stage. The conservative/aggressive scenario describes intended behavior, not proof of its realized category, adoption date or implementation.
Keep the innovation-decision process separate: knowledge, persuasion, decision, implementation and confirmation. For this hypothetical local proposal, knowledge/persuasion and planned evaluation are supportable; approved sandbox access does not prove a decision to adopt the application or confirmation of results. Separate the course's new-adopter bell curve from cumulative S-curve use. Do not fit a Bass model or forecast saturation without a time series and a defensible eligible market.
The slides' 2.5%/13.5% and 16% take-off/chasm references are conceptual model/communication heuristics. They are not a universal empirical law of irreversibility or a measured threshold for these data. S1 reports agent scaling in functions among survey respondents; S2 reports any-AI use among US firms. Neither is cumulative adoption of this application in a defined eventual adopter population. Vendor wording moving from novelty to usefulness may signal persuasion strategy, not proof of the chasm crossing. Say expressly when chasm crossing is UNDETERMINED. A stalled or discontinued adoption path remains possible.

## Required five-factor assessment
Always give the following five named factors at the APPLICATION level, with supporting case/source, inference status and missing evidence. Avoid a repeated generic adjective or an invented overall score.
- Relative advantage: incremental quality, time or total cost versus manual review and deterministic checks, including human checking and false corrections. Local advantage remains unmeasured.
- Compatibility: fit with approved source tables, reporting ownership, data handling and existing controls. Read-only tools may fit part of the workflow; production permissions remain unresolved.
- Complexity: integration, permissions, reliability, staff skills and maintaining audit trails. S3 can inform plausible mechanisms, not a measured industry-wide diffusion effect.
- Trialability: the explicitly approved isolated synthetic/public-data sandbox permits reversible learning. Trialability of a synthetic example does not establish production transfer or approval.
- Observability: reviewable chart/source discrepancy traces and comparable peer results could make value visible. Vendor demos and individual productivity reports are weaker than repeatable application outcomes. No such local measurements are supplied.
State which factor is the most consequential current inhibitor and why; credible observable relative advantage/peer evidence may dominate, with compatibility and complexity interacting. Treat this ordering as an interpretation, not measured causal attribution.

## Actors and ecosystem
Identify change agents, opinion leaders and change aides in a defined social system. In these cases, a proposed implementation lead can organize learning as change agent, a trusted reporting/controls owner could influence peers as opinion leader, and analyst trainers could support users as change aides. These are suggested roles, not named people who have actually adopted or diffused the agent. Vendors and professional networks may communicate demonstrations; promotion alone is not proof of adoption. S4's protocol can reduce some connection friction in principle; no measured market penetration or universal compliance follows from its existence. Data quality, approved tools, budget, human expertise and trustworthy peer references are complementary assets. Explicitly separate observed evidence from plausible accelerators/inhibitors.

## Confidence, abstention and handoff
Separate confidence in source-stated survey results from confidence in transferring them to this application/peer group and from local timing judgment. Include the strongest contrary evidence and at least one weak/ambiguous diffusion claim. Record source age, narrow geography and application mismatch explicitly. Missing matched adoption/use data requires qualification; do not force an early-majority or zero-adoption conclusion. No numeric confidence probability is warranted.
Handoff to the Adoption Agent: strongest valid broad diffusion signal; missing evidence for this application; most important inhibitor; posture-sensitive timing; and a caution that diffusion popularity is not readiness or value. Monitor repeated use among matched peers with a denominator and date, quality/review-cost outcomes, changes in approved access and discontinuance. Proposed review intervals/thresholds are management choices, not Rogers' empirical constants. Preserve abstention and evidence scope in orchestration.

Do not call the market/application diffusion stage "pre-confirmation" or derive it from this unimplemented local proposal. Knowledge-to-confirmation is an organization-level decision process, distinct from market diffusion. When application penetration is missing, write APPLICATION DIFFUSION UNDETERMINED. Use CHASM UNDETERMINED when evidence does not resolve it. Do not rate observability high merely because logs could be built; distinguish design potential from observed outcomes.
