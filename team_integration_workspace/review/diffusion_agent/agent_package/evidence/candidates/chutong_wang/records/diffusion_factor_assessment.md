# Diffusion factor assessment

## Three-level position
General agentic AI: the August 2026 original survey supplies evidence of uneven organizational scaling, with important size/function differences in its sample. It does not measure an eventual adopter population or prove a single Rogers category. An early-market/expansion interpretation is a qualified inference. Specific agentic chart-to-source checking: matching penetration and sustained-use data are missing. Comparable US mid-sized financial peer diffusion and chasm crossing remain undetermined. Missing evidence is not zero adoption.

## Five application factors
| Factor | Assessment and mechanism | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relative advantage | Proposed reduction in review effort or better discrepancy detection could motivate use. Manual and deterministic checks remain comparators. | No local comparison or demonstrated advantage; include false corrections and full human review cost. |
| Compatibility | Read-only approved tables and human report ownership can fit existing practice. Sensitive production access remains unresolved. | Case assumptions, not observed organization results. |
| Complexity | Connecting tools, maintaining provenance and handling model errors can raise effort. Limited resources constrain depth. | S3 is design guidance; magnitude as a diffusion inhibitor is inferred. |
| Trialability | An explicitly approved isolated synthetic/public-data sandbox permits reversible learning. | Does not authorize live enterprise use or prove production transfer. |
| Observability | Source-linked discrepancy traces and comparable peer outcomes could make benefits credible. | No local outcomes/peer installed-base dataset supplied; publicity and demos are weaker substitutes. |

The most consequential present gap is credible observable relative advantage for this application. Compatibility and complexity interact with that gap. This is an analytic priority, not a measured causal ranking or additive score.

## Actors and complementary assets
A proposed implementation lead could act as change agent, a trusted reporting/controls owner as opinion leader and analyst trainers as change aides. No named actor is claimed to have adopted or changed others' behavior. Vendors/professional networks communicate capabilities; trustworthy matched references would better reduce uncertainty. MCP is a protocol example that could reduce connection friction in principle. Approved access, data quality, operational skills and budget are complementary assets. None independently proves adoption rates or readiness.
