# Weak and ambiguous diffusion claims

**Claim under review:** 33.9% of Finance and Insurance firms used AI in the May 2026 BTOS snapshot and 40% of the large-organization survey respondents reported agent scaling, so agentic chart checking must be above the course's 16% tipping point and irreversibly mainstream.

**Why it fails:** S2 measures AI in any business function, not agentic chart checking. S1 measures respondents' organizations scaling agents in one or more functions across countries and industries, not the matching financial peer population. The figures have different sampling units, denominators, geography, fieldwork and definitions. The size of this hypothetical firm's revenue is unknown. They cannot be merged or inserted into a cumulative Rogers curve. Neither supplies a measured eventual-adopter denominator for this application. Vendor usefulness messaging is communication evidence, not a chasm test.

**Age and transfer:** S2's fieldwork ends in May 2026. Its October access date does not make it an October prevalence estimate. It is still useful broad-AI background, but cannot confidently establish the current chart-agent stage. The November 2025 wording change also constrains historical trend comparisons. S1's newer fieldwork improves general context without resolving the application mismatch.

**Corrected inference:** there is a valid broad scaling signal within S1's sample. Specific application and comparable-peer stage/chasm remain unknown. The strongest next evidence would be repeated use of this defined application among a defined eligible peer population, measured on a known date, with discontinuance and outcomes distinguished from intentions and demos.
