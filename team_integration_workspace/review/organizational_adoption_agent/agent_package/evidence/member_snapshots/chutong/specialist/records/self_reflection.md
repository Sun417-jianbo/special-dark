# Self Reflection

The strongest lesson in this analysis is that permission and preparation answer different questions. An approved sandbox removes one barrier, but it does not show who will review questionable corrections, how much time they have, or who takes over when the main reviewer is absent. I would ask for evidence of those arrangements before describing the organization as ready to learn responsibly.

I also distinguish missing evidence from a demonstrated weakness. The baseline does not establish that analysts oppose the tool or that management refuses to fund it. It leaves those questions open. Treating every unknown as failure would make the assessment unfair, while treating it as a strength would hide the very work management needs to do. The useful output is a concrete question, a responsible role and a way to close the uncertainty.

My judgment is to make readiness specific to a scope and a period. A team may have enough protected time for a short PoC while lacking evidence that ongoing review and support are funded. I would preserve that difference in the final intake and handoff, so that a promising technical result cannot silently become permission for routine use. The next management review should examine whether the people, workflow and funding can sustain the application as well as whether the application works.
